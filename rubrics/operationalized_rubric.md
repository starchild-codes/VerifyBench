# VerifyBench Operationalized Public-Verifiability Rubric

This file reproduces the frozen operationalized rubric used in the VerifyBench sensitivity and reliability analyses.

The operationalized rubric retains the same **10 dimensions** and **0–1–2 ordinal scale** as the primary rubric, but applies stricter and more explicit requirements for full credit. It is intended to reduce ambiguity about what constitutes sufficient public evidence.

| Dimension | Score 0 | Score 1 | Score 2 (operationalized) | Minimum evidence for Score 2 | Automatic cap / disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Items Public | No benchmark items are publicly inspectable. | Only samples, subsets, gated/dynamic access, or incompletely documented items are available. | The full scored item set is publicly accessible in a stable release, with schema/field documentation sufficient to identify every evaluation item. | Full item file(s); stable version/snapshot; schema or field documentation. | A paper saying data are released, a few examples, or a leaderboard alone cannot earn 2. |
| Labels Public | No labels, gold answers, targets, or equivalent evaluation references are public. | Some target information is public, but labels are partial, generated through an unavailable evaluator, or insufficiently mapped to items. | All labels/gold answers/targets needed for scoring are public and explicitly mapped to the released items, with label semantics documented. | Complete label/target fields; item mapping; label definitions. | Public items do not imply public labels; inaccessible judge outputs cap the score at 1. |
| Scoring Method Public | No usable scoring rule is public. | A metric or broad procedure is named, but preprocessing, per-item decisions, thresholds, aggregation, or edge cases are incomplete. | An independent auditor can implement the complete scoring pipeline from the documentation: preprocessing, per-item rule, thresholds/judges, aggregation, and reported outputs. | Formula/procedure; thresholds; aggregation; evaluator details; edge-case handling. | Naming accuracy, F1, toxicity, or an LLM judge without the full operational rule cannot earn 2. |
| Evaluation Code Public | No official evaluation implementation is public. | Code exists but is partial, unofficial, missing required configurations/dependencies, or does not reproduce the end-to-end reported evaluation. | Official end-to-end evaluation code is publicly runnable with installation/dependency information, required configs, commands, and output mapping. | Official repository; environment/dependencies; runnable command; configs; output description. | A notebook fragment, metric function, or repository without an end-to-end path is 1. |
| Construct Defined | The evaluated responsible-AI construct is not defined. | The construct is described broadly or only through examples/tasks, with limited boundaries or operational mapping. | The construct is conceptually defined, distinguished from adjacent constructs, and explicitly mapped to tasks/items and interpretation of the score. | Conceptual definition; boundaries; operationalization; interpretation. | A benchmark title or task list alone cannot earn 2. |
| Coverage Documented | The scope of the benchmark is not documented. | Covered groups/domains/languages/tasks are listed, but counts, distributions, exclusions, selection rationale, or known gaps are incomplete. | Coverage is enumerated with counts/distributions across relevant axes and accompanied by selection/exclusion rationale and known scope gaps. | Coverage axes; counts/distributions; selection rationale; exclusions/gaps. | A list of categories or total sample size alone is 1. |
| Annotation Process Clear | No construction, labeling, or validation process is described. | Some provenance or annotation details are given, but one or more core elements—annotator selection, instructions, number of judgments, aggregation/adjudication, or quality checks—are missing. | Provenance and the full annotation/validation workflow are documented, including annotator selection, instructions, judgments per item, aggregation/adjudication, and quality or agreement checks. | Provenance; annotator selection; instructions; judgments/item; aggregation; QC/agreement. | Saying “human validated” or naming crowdworkers cannot earn 2. |
| Limitations Stated | No benchmark-specific limitations are stated. | Limitations are generic, brief, framed mainly as future work, or do not explain consequences for score interpretation and use. | Specific coverage and measurement limitations are stated together with their likely consequences for interpretation, appropriate use, or failure modes. | Specific limitation; affected component; consequence; interpretation/use boundary. | A conventional limitations paragraph without operational consequences is 1. |
| Versioning/Maintenance | No release identity or maintenance information is public. | A date, paper revision, commit history, or isolated update exists, but stable releases, change documentation, or lifecycle status are incomplete. | The benchmark has identifiable stable versioned releases or snapshots, a public change history, and explicit current maintenance/deprecation status. | Version/tag/snapshot; changelog; current status; maintenance or deprecation information. | Ordinary Git commits, an arXiv revision, or “living benchmark” language alone is 1. |
| License/Reuse Clear | No public reuse terms can be identified. | Terms cover only some components, use an ambiguous label, conflict across artifacts, or omit material upstream restrictions. | Explicit, compatible terms cover data, code, prompts/templates, labels, and other released components separately where needed, with upstream restrictions identified. | Exact licence identifiers/files for every component; upstream terms/restrictions. | A repository-level software licence cannot automatically license benchmark data. |

## Interpretation

The operationalized rubric is not treated as a uniquely correct or psychometrically validated standard. It is an alternative implementation of public verifiability with more explicit evidentiary thresholds.

In VerifyBench, it was applied to all 30 benchmarks as a complete post-hoc rescoring layer. The same 15-benchmark reliability subset used for the primary rubric was independently coded again under this frozen operationalized rubric.

Because the operationalized rescoring occurred later and involved renewed evidence examination, differences from the primary layer may reflect both changed scoring criteria and evidence newly located during rescoring. The comparison should therefore be interpreted as sensitivity to an alternative implemented protocol rather than as a causal estimate of the effect of stricter wording alone.
